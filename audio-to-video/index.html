Convert audio to video

Milani gives a voice recording, a podcast, or a song a picture, then saves that as a video. The picture reacts to the sound. The art is code written by Reza Milani, not a clip from an AI video model.

Open the converter

The editor is the tool. This page explains what it does with audio, and the button opens it.

What you are converting

You already have the sound. Milani does not write a song from your speech, and it does not invent footage. It pictures the audio you give it, so the result is something you can post: a square, a 4:5 post, a 9:16 story, or a 16:9 frame.

Three kinds of input work:

  • Record in the browser. The microphone stays on your device. Press Record, speak or play the sound, then stop.
  • Upload an audio file: a voice note, a podcast episode, a song, or an exported mix.
  • Upload a video file when you only want its soundtrack. Milani reads the audio and builds a new picture.

A pair of handles on the waveform chooses the passage that goes into the video. A free export uses the first 30 seconds of that passage and keeps a small “Made with Milani” mark. Collectors who minted Reza Milani’s art on Verse can export up to 5 minutes, without the mark.

How to convert an audio file

  1. Open the editor and leave it on Voice video.
  2. Record with the microphone, or upload an audio file or a video file. Milani uses the soundtrack.
  3. Drag the handles on the waveform so the video uses the passage you care about.
  4. Pick a template, a waveform, or a generative artwork. Add a title, and turn speech into subtitles if you want the words on screen.
  5. Press Make my video. The file is encoded on your device. Save the MP4, or WebM if this browser cannot make MP4.

Pick the picture

The same audio can look very different. A podcast layout puts your photo, a title, and the words in a frame made for speech. A waveform or equalizer draws the shape of the sound. A generative artwork, such as Night River or Fern, moves with the level and the pitch. If the sound is music and you want several artworks cut on the beat, use the music visualizer instead of a single picture.

For a spoken episode, start with the Podcast template. For a voice note that should feel like a poster, the Post templates are a direct fit. You can still drag the photo, the title, and the subtitles until they sit where you want them.

Subtitles, on your device

Speech can become text in the browser. The fast speech model is about 80 MB and downloads once; a larger model is there when you want more accuracy. After that, recognition can run offline. Languages include English, Spanish, French, German, Arabic, Persian, Chinese, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, and others. Every line stays editable, including the time it appears.

Nothing in that step sends your audio to a server. The model comes down. The recording does not go up.

Questions

What audio can I convert?

A recording you make in the browser, an audio file you upload, or the soundtrack of a video file. That covers a voice note, a podcast episode, a song, or a clip you already posted somewhere else.

Does the converter upload my audio?

No. The waveform, the picture, subtitles, and the finished file are made in your browser. Your audio stays on your device. Subtitles download a speech model once; after that, recognition can run offline.

Do I get an MP4?

Yes, when the browser can encode MP4, which current Chrome, Edge, and Safari usually can. If it cannot, Milani saves WebM and says so on the page, because X, Instagram, and TikTok expect MP4.

Can I turn a podcast into a video with subtitles?

Yes. Upload the episode, choose the Podcast template or any other layout, and press Turn my speech into text. You can edit every line, move it, and change the font. A free export is the first 30 seconds.
